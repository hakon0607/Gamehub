# Data processing agreements

## Do you need to sign one with your users?

No — not as things stand. A data processing agreement (Article 28) is needed
when you process personal data **on behalf of someone else**. GameHub does
not: the user's data stays on their own machine, and the statistics are
processed for your own purpose, with their consent, which makes you a
controller rather than a processor.

There is therefore no DPA template here to hand out, and no "GameHub for
business" terms.

## Do you need agreements with your providers?

Yes, and they already exist. Vercel and Neon both act on your instructions
and are processors; both make their data processing terms part of their
standard terms of service, which you accepted when you signed up. Keep a copy
in your own records — see `THIRD-PARTY-SERVICES.md`.

## When this changes

The day a company deploys GameHub to its employees and asks you to sign a
DPA, three things become true at once:

1. You may be processing their employees' data on their behalf, which makes
   you a processor for that data and puts you inside Article 28.
2. That is a commercial relationship, which ends the Cyber Resilience Act
   exemption for non-commercial free software.
3. You are making promises about availability and security to an organisation
   that may hold you to them.

Do not sign anything at that point without advice. It is a genuinely
different product with genuinely different obligations, and "we already had
a privacy policy" does not cover it.
